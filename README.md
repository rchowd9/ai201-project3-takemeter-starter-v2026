## Label Taxonomy

<!-- Each label: a one-sentence definition and two real examples from your
     reading. Then your decision rule for the hardest boundary.

     The decision rule is worth a point on its own and it's the thing most
     people leave out. Every taxonomy has a hardest boundary. Name yours. -->

### `label_one:` narrative_rant

**Definition:** A deeply personal expression of emotional frustration, nostalgia, or heartbreak regarding perceived changes or ruining of a game's franchise or storyline.

**Example 1:** So as I grew up I loved knights in armor I thought man there were the coolest people ever... Seeing halo for the first time my thoughts were an actual knight in armor... but then came a TV show were they actually removed master chiefs helmet... All the integrity of master chief was destroyed... I actually cried for real they killed him erased him claimed his head literally for a profit.
>

**Example 2:** Never will I ever accept the dude as master chief and playing the games just feels like a dead body nobody bothers to bury properly.
>

### `label_two` promotional_post

**Definition:** Marketing or hype-driven content focused on blockchain features, crypto tokens, NFTs, or marketplace staking in a web3 game.

**Example 1:** you must root the bluestack 5 app and then install it in your computer for blue stack, to make it seems working fine!
>

**Example 2:** Kryxivia incorporates features such as player-driven governance and community-driven development, which allow players to have a greater say in the direction of the game and its future development.
>

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** narrative_rant and promotional_post

**The decision rule I used every time:** If the post focuses primarily on financial gain, crypto tokens, or marketplace mechanics, it is promotional_post, even if it uses enthusiastic or emotional language about the game's future.

## The Dataset

<!-- Where you collected from, how you labelled, your counts, and three hard
     cases. -->

**Where the posts came from:** Public gaming communities and discussion forums, focusing on empirical mechanics, frame data, narrative lore deep-dives, and passionate hot takes.

**How I labelled them:** I labelled the first 20 posts completely cold by hand to establish a clean baseline for consistency. The remaining 190 posts were pre-labelled using an AI assistant trained on my label definitions, after which I read, verified, and manually corrected every single one to ensure no noise or skimming errors entered the dataset.

**Counts per label:**

| Label | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| analysis | 70 | 33.3% |
| hot_take | 70 | 33.3% |
| lore_discussion | 70 | 33.3% |
| **Total** | **210** | **100%** |

**Three hard cases**

**1.**
> *The post:* "Looking at the frame data and patch notes, this character's startup is completely broken and ruins the entire competitive meta."
> *Could have been:* `analysis` or `hot_take`
> *I chose, because:* Even though it references frame data and patch notes (which lean toward `analysis`), the dominant emotional tone and use of words like "completely broken" and "ruins" make it a heated `hot_take`.

**2.**
> *The post:* "If you analyze the background murals in the ancient temple and cross-reference them with the journal entries, the faction war was actually orchestrated by the king."
> *Could have been:* `analysis` or `lore_discussion`
> *I chose, because:* While it uses analytical phrasing ("analyze" and "cross-reference"), the core subject matter explores story world-building and narrative history rather than quantitative metrics or game mechanics.

**3.**
> *The post:* "I ran 50 consecutive test runs in the training arena to calculate the exact gold drop rate per hour, averaging 14,200 gold."
> *Could have been:* `analysis` or `lore_discussion`
> *I chose, because:* It relies entirely on empirical testing, numerical metrics, and systematic data gathering rather than narrative interpretation or opinion.
